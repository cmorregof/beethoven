Overview

Claims that one composer borrowed a melody from another usually rest on a resemblance that looks too close to be chance. This project asks how often such resemblances arise by chance in the first place, and builds a test whose false-positive rate is fixed in advance.

  • At eight notes (intervals and rhythm class), … of windows still recur in some other work.
  • With thresholds that keep the false-positive rate near 5 %, sensitivity on documented cases of formal and thematic modelling is about …: the test can flag close, literal sharing but cannot detect diffuse modelling.
  • The confirmatory protocol is preregistered and frozen at commit …. It has not been run.

The question

A musicological claim holds that the Fifth Symphony of Ludwig van Beethoven (op. 67, 1808) draws on the music Luigi Cherubini wrote in revolutionary Paris in the 1790s; the passages usually cited are the opening of the Hymne au Panthéon and the coda of the overture to Eliza, both from 1794.

Stated as a testable hypothesis (H1 of the preregistration): op. 67 shares with Cherubini's works composed before 1808 more eight-note windows of low documentary frequency than a background model of the 1750–1830 repertoire, built without Beethoven or Cherubini, predicts.

This page does not answer the question. The test has not been run, and the two Cherubini works named in the hypothesis have no symbolic edition yet: they must be transcribed by hand before the case can be evaluated.

Method

  1. Windows. Each voice is reduced to a sequence of notes; a window is a run of n consecutive notes, encoded either by its intervals alone (IV) or by intervals plus a coarse rhythm class (IVR).
  2. Base rates. Five nested definitions of the famous four-note motif (D1–D5) are counted across the corpus and compared with a null model that shuffles intervals and durations within each voice.
  3. Uniqueness. For each window length, how many windows of a work also appear in some other work (Pcross), and how likely two random works are to share one (Ppair).
  4. Background model. For a window q and a work B, the expected number of occurrences E = (windows in B) × p(q), where p mixes a Kneser–Ney model of the composer's idiom with one of the period stratum (weight λ). Documentary frequency DF is the share of works in the stratum, excluding both composers, that contain q.
  5. Thresholds. Cut-offs for DF and E are fixed on pairs of works with and without documented influence, never on the target, so that the false-positive rate is about 5 %.
  6. Confirmatory test (not yet run). Count the windows of each Cherubini work that occur in op. 67 with DF below the threshold and compare with 1000 matched null pairs.
